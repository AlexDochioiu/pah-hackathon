## X (thread)
1. We gave two robot arms foam swords and an AI that makes up stories on the spot.
   You say "pirates on Mars." It tells the story out loud. The arms act it out.
   Interrupt it anytime and the story changes mid-sentence. 🧵 {VIDEO}
2. How it works: a LiveKit voice agent improvises the story and drops stage cues like [left:slash] into the
   narration. Each cue fires a gesture on the arm at the moment the word is spoken.
3. The arms are two i2rt YAM 6-axis arms. Every gesture is checked in simulation first: nothing hits the table
   and the actors never collide. (We learned that one the hard way. RIP the e-stop button.)
4. Best part: interruptions. Speak and the arms freeze mid-pose, like actors who forgot their lines. Then the
   narrator rolls with it.
5. Built in 30 hours at @MakerMods Physical AI Hacks at Founders Inc SF with @livekit. Try it / watch: {LINK}

## LinkedIn
This weekend at MakerMods Physical AI Hacks (Founders Inc, San Francisco), we built {NAME}: robot puppet theatre
improvised live by AI.

Ask for any story. A voice agent built on LiveKit makes it up as it goes and narrates it, while two robot arms
act it out with props: a sword fight, a bow, a dramatic death. Interrupt at any moment ("make the dragon
friendly") and the arms freeze, the narrator reacts, and the story takes a new direction.

What we learned:
• Real-time voice changes how a robot feels. Latency and interruption handling matter more than the model.
• We started with a general robot policy for manipulation, then switched to direct control with every motion
  checked in simulation, because a performance has to be reliable every take.
• Robots are funnier than they have any right to be.
